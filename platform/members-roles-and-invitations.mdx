Members belong to an [organization](/platform/organizations-and-workspaces), not to a Receipt workspace. You manage them on Organization settings → **Members**, described in the app as:

> `Manage organization members, pending invitations, permissions, and access status.`

## The three roles

Receipt uses the default role set from its authentication layer. There are exactly three roles and no custom ones.

| Role | Organization | Members | Invitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Update and delete the organization | Create, update and remove members | Create and cancel invitations |
| **Admin** | Update the organization | Create, update and remove members | Create and cancel invitations |
| **Member** | Read only | — | — |

Whoever creates an organization becomes its owner.

<Note>
Owner and admin are not distinguished by Receipt's own admin check: every place Receipt asks "is this person an admin?" — guardrails, organization skills, policies, organization knowledge, the Slack and Teams installs, Stripe checkout and workspace mutations — accepts both identically. The difference between the two is deleting the organization, which only an owner can do.
</Note>

Only **Admin** and **Member** are selectable in the interface. Owner cannot be assigned through **Change role**, and the last owner is protected from both a role change and removal.

## Who can open organization settings

This is the rule that surprises people. Every page under `/organization/settings` is owner and admin only, with one exception: paths that match the MCP gateway rule, which covers `/organization/settings/mcp-gateway` and `/organization/settings/workspaces`, are open to **any** organization member.

So a plain member can open **Workspaces** and the **MCP Gateway**, and cannot open Members, Billing, Usage, BYOK, Security, Policies or the rest.

<Warning>
There is no access-denied screen. A user without the role for a page is silently redirected to the app root, so a settings link that appears to "do nothing" is the access gate refusing you. While your role is still resolving the page shows `Checking workspace access`, and the client-side gate fails closed — during that window it denies rather than allows.
</Warning>

The route gate is not the only check. Each feature enforces its own authorization on the server independently, with its own refusal, such as `Only organization owners or admins can manage guardrails.` or `Only organization owners or admins can manage policies.`

## The Members page

The table has the columns **User**, **Email**, **Role** and **Status**, plus a row action menu. There is a filter box placeholder `Filter members...`, the loading state `Loading members and invitations...`, and the empty state `No members or pending invitations found.` Pending invitations are merged into the first page of the table and de-duplicated against active member emails, so an invited address does not appear twice.

Status is one of three badges:

| Badge | Meaning | Tooltip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `active` | A member with access | — |
| `pending` | An invitation not yet accepted | `Invitation has not been accepted yet.` |
| `restricted` | Access is withheld | The recorded reason code, or `Access is restricted.` |

Row actions are **Change role** (an **Admin** / **Member** submenu), **Remove member**, and **Cancel invitation** on pending rows. Your own row's menu is disabled — you cannot change or remove yourself from this table. The actions toast `Role updated.`, `Member removed.` and `Invitation cancelled.`, or the matching `Failed to …` message.

## Inviting people

**Invite members** opens the **Invite member** dialog, described as `Send an invitation to join this organization.` Each row has an **Email address** field with the placeholder `colleague@example.com` and a role select offering **Member** (the default) or **Admin**. **Add another** adds a row, up to **ten addresses per batch**, and **Send invitation** submits them.

Receipt sends the batch as one API call per address, in sequence, so a batch can partly succeed. The outcomes you can see:

- `Invitation sent.` or `{n} invitations sent.`
- `{n} invited; {m} failed. {first error}` when some addresses fail
- `Enter at least one email address.` when you submit an empty form

### What the invitee gets

The invitation email's subject is:

> `You're invited to join {organizationName}`

It carries an **Accept invitation** button pointing at `/auth/accept-invitation/<invitationId>`, and labels the inviter and the organization. **Invitations expire after 48 hours.**

<Info>
On a self-hosted deployment with no mail transport configured, nothing is emailed. The dialog reports `Invitation created. Copy the signup link below.` (or `{n} invitations created. Copy the signup links below.`) and shows a panel reading `Copy these signup links and share them directly with teammates.` with one `/auth/sign-up?invitationId=<id>` link per invitee. Sharing those links is then the only way the invitation reaches anyone.
</Info>

### Accepting

A signed-out invitee opening the link is sent to sign-up with the invitation attached. Receipt looks up the invited address and **pre-fills the email field and makes it read-only**, with the hint `This invitation is tied to this email address.` Trying to continue with a different address gives:

> `Use the email address that received the invitation to continue.`

A signed-in invitee instead sees a card titled **Organization invitation** — `Review this invitation before joining the organization in Receipt.` — showing who invited them, with **Join organization** and **Decline**. On success it reads **Welcome aboard!** and `You have successfully joined the organization. Redirecting to your workspace…`, then waits three seconds before moving on. Failures surface as `Invalid invitation`, `Failed to load invitation`, `Failed to accept invitation` or `Failed to reject invitation`.

## Seats

An organization's seat count comes from its plan entitlement, covered in [Usage, plans, and billing](/platform/usage-and-billing). Capacity is checked when you invite: Receipt counts **active members, plus pending invitations, plus the new invite** against the seat count. Pending invitations occupy seats even though nobody has accepted them.

An invite that would overflow fails with HTTP 403 and this message, surfaced per address in the invite dialog:

> `Only {n} users can access this workspace.`

An organization that is over its limit is flagged, and the billing and sidebar surfaces show it with these strings: **Seat allocation** and `{activeMembers} of {seatCount} seats`, plus **Over seat limit** and **No paid seat assigned** on affected members. If you select fewer seats than you have active members, the billing page warns: `You selected {n} seats for {m} active members. Everyone keeps access until period end, and extra members will be auto-restricted after renewal.`

## Sharing a single workspace

Sharing one Receipt workspace from the Workspaces page uses a different mechanism from an organization invitation. It is a signed `wsinv_` token with a 48-hour lifetime, delivered as an `/auth/accept-invitation/<token>` link, and it only works for someone who is **already a member of the organization**.

If the address you enter does not belong to an organization member, Receipt answers `new_user` rather than sharing: invite that person to the organization first, then share the workspace with them. If they already have access, it answers `already_has_access`.
